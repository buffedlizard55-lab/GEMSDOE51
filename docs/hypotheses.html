H51-1 — Stage-A geodetic strain-budget gate: top-half tiles by geodetic strain magnitude, after the brief's literal deficit rule was tested and refuted

rank 1 · status: REFUTED as a locator, then replaced. (a) Blocked holdout: the deficit's approved tiles concentrate held-out catalogue labels with lift 0.949, only 1/4 folds above 1 (rho -0.338). (b) Independent instrument 1 (lidar scarp intensity, in-fill H19-4 layer): rho -0.048. (c) Independent instrument 2 (off-catalogue SGMC fault density): rho -0.228. The adopted substitute, geodetic strain magnitude, is +0.376 (top-quartile lift 1.393) on the lidar instrument and -0.166 on the SGMC instrument: a split decision, reported as a split (IR-51-04).

Layers

  • training_features.tif band 4 = geod_2ndinv (second invariant of the geodetic strain-rate tensor)
  • training_features.tif band 7 = geod_shearrate, band 8 = geod_dilaterate (comparison rules)
  • external/gdr_qfaults_traces.csv (INGENIOUS/GDR-1391 slip rates + clipped trace lengths)
  • external Siler (2022) slip-tendency table (per-sense dips, USGS ScienceBase 6296974d)

Signature targeted

Tile-level mass balance between what geodesy requires and what mapped faults can supply. 116 tiles of 25 km. Median geodetic second invariant 21.03 ns/yr; median mapped-fault equivalent 6.00 ns/yr; median ratio 0.455; median deficit 0.545.

Formula / mechanism

eps_geo = (1/(2*A_tile)) * sum_k L_k*u_k/sin(dip_k) -- Kreemer et al. (2000) EPSG 52:765-770 eq. (3), the fault-slip variant of the Kostrov (1974) summation; dip by slip sense from Siler (2022): normal 61.9 deg, right-lateral 85.8, left-lateral 81.6, unspecified 62.8 (per-sense median of 83,234 measured rows; the overall median across all rows is 64.3).

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

A tile whose geodetic strain is not matched by mapped-fault moment is, by construction, a place where structure exists that the catalogue does not contain. The gate is deliberately coarse (58 of 116 tiles, 2,537,841 px = 49% of the footprint) so that Stage B, not Stage A, decides placement.

How it differs from everything already implemented

No sibling repository used fault slip rates at all. GEMSDOE26 tested the geodetic deformation field as a *feature* (dilcond-oof, 0.1223); here the geodetic field is a physically derived budget whose residual is measured and, when found wanting, replaced by the validated statistic rather than smuggled into the emission.

Validation

REFUTED as a locator, then replaced. (a) Blocked holdout: the deficit's approved tiles concentrate held-out catalogue labels with lift 0.949, only 1/4 folds above 1 (rho -0.338). (b) Independent instrument 1 (lidar scarp intensity, in-fill H19-4 layer): rho -0.048. (c) Independent instrument 2 (off-catalogue SGMC fault density): rho -0.228. The adopted substitute, geodetic strain magnitude, is +0.376 (top-quartile lift 1.393) on the lidar instrument and -0.166 on the SGMC instrument: a split decision, reported as a split (IR-51-04).

Expected gain vs cost

Cost: 2 h of table work + a 116-tile instrument. Expected gain as a locator: 0 (refuted); gain as a *mask* that removes 51% of the emission domain at no measured cost in credit: kept. This is the single most important negative result in this repository.

Signature targeted

HistGradientBoosting (220 iters, lr 0.08, 31 leaves) on 44 channels; 4 spatially blocked folds with a 600 m boundary band removed from training and scoring; belief field emitted as sparse 1-px dots at 3 px minimum separation, sized at 4% of the candidate area.

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

A scarp-sharpness ensemble detects the *physical* fault trace, not its administrative record.

H51-3 — Literal strain-budget DEFICIT (geodetic minus mapped-fault moment) as the Stage-A prior: implemented, tested, FALSIFIED

rank 3 · status: FALSIFIED on all three available instruments (blocked holdout label density lift 0.949, 1/4 folds; rho -0.048 on lidar scarp intensity; rho -0.228 on off-catalogue SGMC density). Retained in the pipeline as a diagnostic column and in the site as the headline negative result.

Layers

  • geod_2ndinv, geod_shearrate, geod_dilaterate (official bands 4/7/8)
  • INGENIOUS/GDR-1391 slip rates x clipped lengths
  • Siler (2022) per-sense dips

Signature targeted

Residual = geodetic second invariant minus the mapped-fault equivalent computed with the published formula. Under the brief it should mark the missing moment. It does not.

Formula / mechanism

—

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

The physical argument is sound in principle (Zeng & Shen 2016 put a large off-fault moment rate in California; 17% of the worldwide GSRM moment rate sits in diffuse zones), which is exactly why the test was run. A sound argument with a negative test result must be reported as a negative result.

How it differs from everything already implemented

No sibling repository tested this rule at all; it comes from the task brief and from the literature cited above. It is reported here in full, including the formula, the numbers, and the two independent instruments that refute it.

Validation

FALSIFIED on all three available instruments (blocked holdout label density lift 0.949, 1/4 folds; rho -0.048 on lidar scarp intensity; rho -0.228 on off-catalogue SGMC density). Retained in the pipeline as a diagnostic column and in the site as the headline negative result.

Expected gain vs cost

Cost: 2 h. Measured gain: 0 as a locator. Value: it prevents a 0.3195-beating strategy from being built on an unvalidated premise.

H51-4 — Per-sense dip and slip-sense weighting inside the Kostrov sum (rigorous version of the Stage-A formula)

rank 4 · status: PARTIAL. Dips measured and applied (normal 61.9, RL 85.8, LL 81.6, unspecified 62.8 deg); the fraction of trace length with a resolved sense is 36% of total length (6,286 of 17,375 km inside the footprint). The full moment-tensor version is NOT TESTED: the joined table has strike and dip but no rake.

Layers

  • Siler (2022) slip- and dilation-tendency tables (83,234 rows with Dip and slip_sense in the footprint)
  • INGENIOUS trace table (868 normal, 87 right-lateral, 76 left-lateral, 95 other)

Signature targeted

Replace the single assumed 60-degree dip with the measured per-sense dip distribution, and (next) build the full unit-moment tensor m_ij from strike and dip rather than the scalar contraction. The tensor version requires a rake, which the joined table does not carry; the strike and dip columns do exist, so a partial tensor is available.

Formula / mechanism

—

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

Not a detection idea: a measurement-quality idea. It changes the *magnitude* of the summed strain but not, to first order, the tile pattern (a scalar multiplier cancels in a quantile gate), so its value is scientific rather than score-bearing - unless the tensor version reveals that strike-slip and normal tiles need different geodetic bands.

How it differs from everything already implemented

The siblings used no slip rates and no dips at all; this is the only place in the family where the trace geometry and the per-sense dips are joined to the official strain-rate bands.

Validation

PARTIAL. Dips measured and applied (normal 61.9, RL 85.8, LL 81.6, unspecified 62.8 deg); the fraction of trace length with a resolved sense is 36% of total length (6,286 of 17,375 km inside the footprint). The full moment-tensor version is NOT TESTED: the joined table has strike and dip but no rake.

Expected gain vs cost

Cost for the partial version: done. Cost for the tensor version: ~3 h given a rake source (none is currently obtainable for free; INGENIOUS carries sense, not rake). Expected score gain: 0 unless the tensor reveals a band choice that the scalar hides. Ranked below the detection ideas for that reason.

H52-1 — Horsetail / fault-termination and step-over geometry completion (structural-setting arm)

rank 1 · status: NOT YET RUN. Must clear the off-catalogue A/B instrument (mean paired DTI > 0, >= 3/4 folds) before it can consume a submission slot; a positive result on the visible catalogue alone is explicitly insufficient (IR-51-06).

Layers

  • visible catalogue trace geometry (labels.tif / existing_faults.tif skeletons)
  • the existing 44-channel belief field (src/gems51/features.py) used only for orientation
  • band 12 det_elev + band 19 det_elev_slope for scarp-horst expression
  • Faulds & Hinz (2015) structural-setting catalogue as the target definition, not as labels

Signature targeted

Multiplicity of sub-parallel lineaments near a trace end. Faulds & Hinz (2015) measured that, of ~250 categorised Great Basin systems, step-overs/relay ramps host ~32 %, fault terminations (horsetails) 25 %, fault intersections 22 %, and that Quaternary faults lie within or near most systems.

Formula / mechanism

For every catalogue trace endpoint, measure the local orientation of the ridge field (Hessian eigenvector, 15-31 px window) and count sub-parallel responses in a 500-1500 m fan past the tip: n_strands(tip) = |{ ridge maxima with |angle diff| <= 20 deg }|. Score = n_strands x (1 - normalized distance along the fan) x end-vs-mid contrast of the belief field.

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

A mapped trace ends where the mapper stopped, not where faulting stops: horsetails and step-overs are precisely the geometry that a 1:250k compilation truncates. The target signature is a spatial ARRANGEMENT (multiple overlapping strands) rather than a stronger per-pixel signal, so it can exist in the data while every single band looks unremarkable at the tip.

How it differs from everything already implemented

Every detector in this family, and both failed sibling experiments (strike-STEERING, AUC 0.4542; horn/gradient variants), acts on a per-pixel magnitude or a global orientation gate. Nothing here has ever scored a pixel by how MANY sub-parallel structures coincide near it. The strike-steering failure refuted orientation as a global gate, not as a local multiplicity statistic.

Validation

NOT YET RUN. Must clear the off-catalogue A/B instrument (mean paired DTI > 0, >= 3/4 folds) before it can consume a submission slot; a positive result on the visible catalogue alone is explicitly insufficient (IR-51-06).

Expected gain vs cost

Cost: ~3 h (reuses the cached belief field and ridge orientation; no new data). Expected gain: the highest of the five - it targets the exact settings that host a third of the region's known (mostly blind) systems, and it needs no new bytes.

H52-2 — Paleo-geothermal discharge deposits (sinter / travertine / tufa) as an independent fault tracer

rank 2 · status: NOT YET RUN. Instrument: off-catalogue A/B (fault systems split A/B). A deposit-only control (emit on deposits regardless of belief) must also be run, to separate deposit proximity from detector skill.

Layers

  • GDR-1391 'Paleo Geothermal Features' shapefile (spring deposits: sinter, travertine, tufa), CC-BY 4.0
  • the detector belief field, restricted to a <= 3 km annulus around each mapped deposit
  • band 17 cond_surf + band 16 ieq_n100a15 for corroboration

Signature targeted

Spring deposits mark where geothermal fluid reached the surface. Their position is controlled by the permeability structure (i.e. faults) that carried the fluid, so a deposit with no mapped fault under it is direct evidence of an unmapped fault or of a fault segment the catalogue truncates.

Formula / mechanism

Proximity field P(x) = exp(-d(x, deposit)/1.5 km); score = belief x P, emitted under the same thin-dot rule inside approved tiles. Deposits are point/line references, so d is an exact Euclidean distance transform.

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

The deposit compilation is independent of the fault catalogue: it was assembled from spring-deposit mapping, not from fault mapping. A deposit is therefore a fault proxy that the evaluation's own masked catalogue does not contain.

How it differs from everything already implemented

No member of the family has ever downloaded the GDR-1391 paleo-geothermal package (the sibling ledger records it as NOT downloaded). Sibling GEMSDOE25/28 used Quaternary volcanics and heat flow, which are heat-source proxies; deposits are FLUID-PATHWAY proxies, a different physical object.

Validation

NOT YET RUN. Instrument: off-catalogue A/B (fault systems split A/B). A deposit-only control (emit on deposits regardless of belief) must also be run, to separate deposit proximity from detector skill.

Expected gain vs cost

Cost: ~2 h including data acquisition; the usable layer is 19.85 MB. Data download is the only risk and it is now specified with a URL and a runner path.

H52-3 — 2-m temperature-probe residual field as a continuous thermal channel

rank 3 · status: NOT YET RUN. Must beat the deposit-only and belief-only controls on the off-catalogue instrument; because probes are surveys (lines), the residual field is spatially biased toward surveyed areas and that bias must be quantified, not assumed away.

Layers

  • GDR-1391 '2m Temperature Probes' package (1.03 MB, CC-BY)
  • band 12 det_elev and band 19 det_elev_slope to remove the elevation/insolation trend
  • bands 5/11/13/18 (gravity/magnetic slope, vertical gradient) as the structural context

Signature targeted

A shallow (2 m) positive temperature residual that is not explained by topography. The INGENIOUS documentation describes these surveys as identifying 'subtle thermal anomalies that may be associated with geothermal systems' - i.e. advective heat from depth, which requires a permeable path.

Formula / mechanism

R(x) = T2m(x) - f(elevation(x), aspect(x)) with f fitted by gradient boosting on the probe locations away from known systems; then score = belief_field x rank(R), smooth at 500 m.

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

The measurement is a physical temperature, not a mapped line. An anomalous residual can only be explained by fluid flow, and flow concentrates along faults; the catalogue is irrelevant to whether the anomaly exists.

How it differs from everything already implemented

Siblings used probe COUNT as a corroboration channel (a tally of surveys near a candidate). Nobody has used the measured temperatures as a continuous field with the topographic trend removed, which is the actual geophysical signal.

Validation

NOT YET RUN. Must beat the deposit-only and belief-only controls on the off-catalogue instrument; because probes are surveys (lines), the residual field is spatially biased toward surveyed areas and that bias must be quantified, not assumed away.

Expected gain vs cost

Cost: ~2 h. Expected gain: medium-high; it is the only independent, measured THERMAL field available free of charge for this area.

H52-4 — Quaternary volcanic vent adjacency as a magnitude-scaled heat-source arm

rank 4 · status: NOT YET RUN. Instrument as above; the magnetic guard is a required control, otherwise the arm is just detecting volcanic rock, which is not the target class.

Layers

  • GDR-1391 'Quaternary Volcanics' shapefile (polygons, CC-BY)
  • band 1 mag_anom, band 2 rtp, band 14 tmi (young volcanic rocks are magnetic)
  • belief field as the placement prior

Signature targeted

Proximity to Quaternary magmatism - the heat source for the highest-enthalpy systems in the Walker Lane / NW Nevada corridor.

Formula / mechanism

Score = belief x sum_v exp(-d(x, vent_v)/w) with w scaled by polygon area (a proxy for erupted volume); add a magnetic-signature check so the arm cannot simply trace the volcanic rock itself.

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

Young volcanic centres are mapped independently of faults and are the surface expression of the heat that drives the system; the faults that feed an associated system are frequently unmapped at 1:250k.

How it differs from everything already implemented

Siblings used quaternary volcanics as a binary distance channel inside a broader stack; this arm makes it a magnitude-scaled, magnetically guarded score. It is ranked below H52-1..3 because siblings have already seen a version of this channel, so the expected marginal gain is smaller.

Validation

NOT YET RUN. Instrument as above; the magnetic guard is a required control, otherwise the arm is just detecting volcanic rock, which is not the target class.

Expected gain vs cost

Cost: ~2 h. Expected gain: medium; the channel is partly covered by existing work.

H52-5 — Multi-depth magnetotelluric conductance gradient (clay-cap geometry) instead of surface conductance

rank 5 · status: NOT YET RUN, and LAST for a reason: the multi-depth MT package is large (the conductance maps are tens of MB), the depth slices are in separate products, and the ScienceBase host is unreachable from this environment, so acquisition risk is highest here.

Layers

  • INGENIOUS MT electrical-conductance maps (GDR-1391; also Peacock & Bedrosian 2022, ScienceBase 62979746d34ec53d276c113b)
  • the official band 17 cond_surf is a single surface value - the new object is the depth series
  • strain bands 4/7/8 for the structural prior

Signature targeted

A vertical conductivity gradient beneath the surface - the classic clay-cap signature of an active upflow zone - rather than a high surface conductance value.

Formula / mechanism

C_ij = conductivity at the i-th depth layer; score = belief x max_i |dC_i/dz| localised to 400-1200 m depth, where the smectite-to-illite transition marks upflow zones; use the vertical gradient of log-conductivity as the feature.

Why it catches a fault missing from the catalogue

The clay cap sits ABOVE and AROUND the upflow, so it is displaced from the fault that feeds it; a fault that is unmapped can still produce a mapped conductivity halo.

How it differs from everything already implemented

The competition's band 17 gives one surface conductance raster; siblings used MT as static layers. The vertical gradient is a different physical object and the one the literature actually uses to locate upflow.

Validation

NOT YET RUN, and LAST for a reason: the multi-depth MT package is large (the conductance maps are tens of MB), the depth slices are in separate products, and the ScienceBase host is unreachable from this environment, so acquisition risk is highest here.

Expected gain vs cost

Cost: high (data acquisition + depth alignment). Expected gain: medium, and it is the arm with the most independent physics behind it if the data can be obtained.
