H-GHigh-angle crossings of independent magnetic and gravity boundariestmi, iso_grav_anomNormalized-convolution gradients at sigma=4 and 8 pixels (400 m, 800 m). At each scale, score axial discordance 1-|dot(g_TMI,g_gravity)|/(|g_TMI||g_gravity|), weighted by both edge magnitudes after robust scaling; suppress negligible edges. The score is a structural proxy, not a mapped fault.Great Basin geothermal systems commonly occur in fault intersections, terminations and transfer structures where permeability can be enhanced. Independent magnetic and gravity boundaries crossing at a high angle may flag a structure intersection that is not expressed as a single continuous surface scarp.This repository's incumbent H-D uses LiDAR scarp-facing coherence; H-E uses co-located basement/conductivity/gravity derivatives. The new operator is high-angle, edge-weighted TMI–gravity discordance at two scales. It is NOT a new family: GEMSDOE26 H26-XEDGE and GEMSDOE27 H28-1 document gradient-orientation agreement; GEMSDOE49 documents magnetic–gravity orientation work. H53 contributed no substantive method.Low-to-moderate and uncertain; independent geophysical boundaries may identify intersections, but regional structures are not necessarily faults or geothermal conduits. No numeric uplift was preregistered; this was an exploratory screen.Low–medium: two existing rasters, two static derived channels, about 9 minutes for the six-fold baseline-arm run on this sandbox; matched-incumbent run costs another several minutes.matched add-on DTI=0.28106; Δ vs H-D=-0.00061; 3/6 folds above; NOT PROMOTED: matched mean proxy DTI delta -0.000606262 versus H-D (3/6 folds higher); no competition slot authorized. H-HPotential-field edge terminations with a conductive relay bridgetmi_hg or iso_grav_anom_hg, depth_to_base_surf, cond_surf, lidar step_max/coh100 as an independent surface checkExtract finite edge segments and endpoints from potential-field and basement-depth gradient ridges; identify spatially offset, approximately aligned terminations separated by 0.3–2 km, with a conductivity anomaly in the intervening bridge. Score only connected, multi-layer-consistent pairs; do not reward a generic edge-crossing or a single high-gradient pixel.Buried fault strands can terminate or transfer displacement beneath basin fill. Relay/transfer zones can host dense fracture networks; a conductive bridge between independently imaged structural terminations could be consistent with fractured or altered material. Conductivity is non-unique (clay, basin fill and lithology are alternatives), so this is a testable proxy, not proof of permeability.H-E in this repository scores local co-location of basement, conductivity and gravity derivatives; H-G scores local high-angle edge crossings. H-H would represent finite edge endpoints and an offset bridge, i.e. segment topology rather than local co-location. Sibling work already explores relays/stepovers, fault tips and geophysical edge agreement (including GEMSDOE32 H33-5); therefore only this exact joint endpoint/bridge operator is a candidate variant, not an untried family.Qualitative, pre-test expectation: potentially moderate localization benefit over single-edge/co-location scores if paired terminations and a conductive relay bridge selectively mark fault-transfer corridors. No numeric DTI uplift is claimed; endpoint pairing and conductivity non-uniqueness make the sign uncertain.Medium–high: edge thinning, graph construction, endpoint pairing, scale/offset controls and leakage-safe blocked ablation.PROPOSED; not implemented or tested.
