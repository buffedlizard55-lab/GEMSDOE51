H-51-GStrike-coherent trace emission: spend mass along the trace, not across itdetector belief field (any arm), multi-scale Frangi vesselness of the belief field (sigma = 1.0, 2.0, 3.5 px), Vanderbrug straight-line accumulator over 12 orientations, 900 m lengthscore = rank(belief)^(1-w) * rank(line-accumulated ridgeness)^w, then greedy selection with a circular exclusion of 2.4 px. The ridge filter answers 'is this pixel on the crest of a linear structure rather than its shoulder', and the accumulator answers 'does that structure persist for 9 pixels in one direction'. Dots are then spread ALONG the surviving crest instead of being packed into the brightest blob.This is a metric-geometry hypothesis, not a geology one, and it is the one the scoring definition forces. TP_w = sum over truth of max over predictions of k(d): a dot 2 px off the crest earns k(2) = 1/3 of a crest dot, and a second dot across the width of the same structure earns nothing extra while costing a full unit of FP. Uncatalogued faults are, by the INGENIOUS step-over evidence (Giddens 2025), long low-relief strands rather than compact anomalies, so the belief field's bright blobs are exactly where the catalogue already is and the belief field's long faint ridges are where it is not.The incumbent emitter is gems51.emission.nms_dots: isotropic greedy non-maximum suppression of the raw belief, blind to orientation and to continuity. Nothing in the repo had ever varied the emission GEOMETRY at matched mass; every previous sweep varied mass or the gate.+0.002 to +0.004 (small but nearly free: no retraining, seconds of CPU)low (one new module, src/gems51/trace_emission.py; no model refit)PENDING-6-FOLD (3-fold pilot: +0.0033 mean, best rule ste_L9_w0.7_s2.4_nothin at 0.2921 vs 0.2888 incumbent)
H-51-FAuto-context stacking: give a per-pixel tree the spatial context a U-Net haslevel-1 belief field, 8 derived context layers: ac_rank, ac_mean05/11/21, ac_max05, ac_ridge, ac_line09, ac_line17Tu & Bai (2010) auto-context. A level-1 HistGradientBoosting model over the 58 physical layers produces a belief map; multi-scale summaries of that map (local means at 500 m / 1.1 km / 2.1 km, local max, Frangi ridgeness, and 900 m / 1.7 km line accumulators) are appended to the raw features and a level-2 model is trained on the union. The level-1 belief used to build level-2 TRAINING rows is cross-fitted over two spatially contiguous slabs (Breiman 1996) so the stack cannot learn to over-trust a belief that already saw the label.The scored object is a trace, not a pixel. The official reference solution buys that context with a U-Net over 128x128 patches; auto-context buys the same statement ('is there a long coherent high-belief lineament through this pixel') for a tree at CPU cost. H-51-JBlakely-Simpson maxspot edges stacked over upward-continuation heightscomp_rtp_mag (reduced-to-pole magnetics), comp_iso_grav_anom (isostatic gravity)Horizontal-gradient magnitude of each potential field, then sub-pixel location of its local maxima by the Blakely & Simpson (1986) 4-direction test, repeated after upward continuation to 250 m / 500 m / 1 km / 2 km. The output layer per field is the continuation height at which the edge is still detected (a depth proxy) plus the maxspot density at each height.This is the USGS's own production method for mapping CONCEALED faults from potential-field grids, and it is how much of the Great Basin basement structure outside the Quaternary-scarp catalogue was first identified. Giddens (2025) reports that gravity was the single most useful geophysical dataset for defining the extent of Quaternary faults in exactly this region, because basin fill conceals them and geothermal alteration suppresses their magnetic signature. A maxspot that persists to 2 km of upward continuation is a deep, long basement structure - precisely the class a scarp-mapped catalogue cannot contain.The shipped stack contains the raw potential fields and generic Hessian/gradient filters of them, but no edge-DETECTION operator, no multi-height continuation, and therefore no depth discrimination. Every existing gravity feature is a single-scale amplitude or gradient.+0.004 to +0.010medium (upward continuation is an FFT per height; 8 FFTs over a 3730x3292 grid plus the 4-direction test, ~5 min, then one detector refit ~25 min for 6 folds)PROPOSED - not implemented this session (ran out of compute budget behind H-51-F and H-51-G). H-51-IRadiometric alteration-ratio lineaments (K/Th and U/Th)comp_rad_k (potassium), comp_rad_th (thorium), comp_rad_u (uranium), comp_rad_tc (total count)Pixelwise log ratios log(K/Th) and log(U/Th), which cancel the common detector/geometry/soil-moisture gain that dominates the raw channels, followed by the same Frangi ridge + line-accumulator pipeline used for the belief field. Thorium is immobile in hydrothermal systems; potassium and uranium are mobile. A linear K/Th high is therefore a mapped-out fluid pathway.A geothermal-fluid conduit alters its wall rock whether or not it has a scarp. This finds the fault by its FLUID history rather than by its topography, so its failure mode is uncorrelated with the scarp-based catalogue's - exactly the kind of independence the Tversky metric rewards, since alpha = 0.2 makes false positives cheap and recall expensive.The repo uses K, Th, U and total count only as raw amplitudes fed straight to the tree. H-51-KMissability inverse-propensity weighting: train on what the mappers would have missedcatalogue (as the label), lid_relief, comp_det_elev, lidar coverage mask, slope, drainage textureFit a second model p(mapped | fault-like) that predicts, among catalogued pixels, how EASY each one was to map (high relief, inside lidar coverage, far from dense vegetation/dune cover). Then re-weight the positive training rows by 1/p. The detector is thereby pushed toward the part of the fault population the mappers systematically under-sampled.The target is defined as the faults the catalogue does NOT contain, so the training label is a biased sample of the truth, biased exactly by mappability.
