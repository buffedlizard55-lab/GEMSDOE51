The biggest limitation, stated first. The file this project has been calling the training labels is byte-identical to the known-fault raster — same sha256, element-wise equal, and equal again to the catalogue this session derived independently from the INGENIOUS compilation. The competition's real training labels have therefore never been available to any GEMSDOE session (IR-51-08). Every model here, including the one that produced the files below, is trained against the visible catalogue as a proxy for the hidden truth. That proxy has been honest enough to produce a verified, unique, portal-legal submission, but it is a proxy, and it is the reason our holdout number is an estimate of our own detector rather than a measurement against the real target. Nothing on this site should be read as a prediction of the leaderboard.

Phase 1 entries are not free. The organizer has stated on the forum that the Phase 2 test set "will use a test set that is updated by expert review of all Phase 1 submissions, so your fault predictions have an impact on final evaluation even if they are not the most performant in Phase 1" (chrisk-dd, 2026-09-23). Every dot in the files below is therefore geologically defensible on its own terms, not just score-optimal: all of them lie outside the 200 m exclusion zone, none sits on a catalogued trace, and the Stage 1 strain-budget argument for why each tile is worth looking at is published on the method page.

What this is

GEMSDOE51 predicts geothermal-indicative faults in the GeoDAWN region that are not in the USGS / INGENIOUS catalogue, and ships them as a competition-legal GeoTIFF. It is a two-stage system: a coarse geodetic strain-budget deficit prior over 10 km tiles (Stage 1), and a fine-scale fault detector whose emitted points are placed only inside the tiles Stage 1 approves (Stage 2). Both stages are scored separately on a preregistered spatially blocked holdout.

The three facts that decide everything

  1. Known faults are masked. DrivenData staff confirmed on 2026-09-16 that pixels corresponding to known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are excluded from evaluation, in both prize rounds. source
  2. "New fault" includes new geometry of an existing system. Continuations, splays and parallel strands count. source
  3. The metric is a budget. From the published definitions, DTI = TP / (0.2·(TP+FP) + 0.8·|G|). The hidden truth size |G| is fixed, so a submission stands or falls on credit earned per unit of emitted mass. The break-even bar for a marginal pixel is c = 0.2·DTI.
