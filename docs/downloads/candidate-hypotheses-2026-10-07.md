## Source checks completed before implementation

- DrivenData's [problem description, layers, metric, and submission format](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) was opened. It describes the target as faults, says the test labels are expert-identified faults absent from the existing public USGS database, identifies the training feature grid as EPSG:32611 at 100 m, lists magnetic/gravity/geodetic/conductivity layers, defines a distance-weighted Tversky metric, and requires one float32 band with probabilities in [0,1].
- The official [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) was opened. It states magnetic data support subsurface-structure/geology mapping and radiometric data support surface geology/soil-composition mapping; it does not by itself prove any raster edge is a fault. The local hash-pinned training-raster descriptions identify the four K1 bands as TMI/Isostatic-gravity horizontal/vertical gradients. A value audit found `tmi_hg` positive over its finite footprint but `iso_grav_anom_hg` spans −15.44 to +12.86; the owner-mirror descriptions do not settle whether the latter is a full gradient magnitude or signed component. K1 therefore uses absolute HG and is explicitly a partial-gradient phase proxy, not a textbook total-horizontal-derivative tilt angle.
- The USGS [blind geothermal systems / Great Basin report](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/discovering-blind-geothermal-systems-great-basin-region-integrated-geologic-and) was opened. It describes a blind-system case involving structural setting and intersecting/terminating gravity gradients, magnetic lows, low resistivity, temperature and geochemistry. This is geological precedent for integrated structural testing, not validation of the H51-K1 pixel operator.
- The public UNR copy of Kreemer et al. (2000), Eq. 3, was checked for the separate Stage-1 formula: [PDF](https://geodesy.unr.edu/publications/Kreemer_et_al_GlobalStrain_2000.pdf). The equation is a fault-segment tensor sum; this source is not a validation of the coarse Stage-1 proxy or the hidden labels.
- The ArcGIS REST schema for [QFaults INGENIOUS layer 0](https://web2.nbmg.unr.edu/arcgis/rest/services/Qfaults/Qfaults_INGENIOUS/MapServer/0?f=pjson) was opened. It contains the fields `SLIPRTNUM` (numeric), `SLIPRT2023`, `SLIPSENSE`, `DIPDIRECT`, and `Shape_Leng`; the service description links the shapefile and field definitions at DOI 10.15121/1881483. The service schema alone does **not** authenticate units for `SLIPRTNUM`. The local trace CSV is a hash-pinned derived owner mirror; its `slip_rate`, `clipped_length_m`, and centroid fields are not a substitute for source field definitions. Stage-1 absolute units remain provisional.
- The keyword-selected sibling prior-art audit covers 582 public owner documents across 54 repositories, not all branches/code/literature. Existing edge-orientation, cross-gradient, step-over, scarps, and habitat families are documented; no global novelty claim is made. See `evidence/sibling_prior_art_audit_20261007.json`.
